AI & data

The software runs on a hub inside the habitat. Sensors and the check-in station feed it; it classifies the crew's state, decides on a response using plain rules, sends the response to the lights, speaker and fan, and logs everything. Health and psychological data never leave the habitat unencrypted.

The loop

  1. Sense — sentiment from the daily check-in, biometrics from a wearable, and cabin readings (CO₂, temperature, humidity, pressure, light).
  2. Classify — a Random Forest model labels the crew state as Neutral, Suspected Illness or Underlying Issue.
  3. Decide — fixed rules with hold times so the habitat does not flicker between states.
  4. Act — lighting color and level, soundscape, fan and greenhouse airflow, activity suggestions.
  5. Log — every cycle goes to an SD card and the dashboard.

More than half of the signal comes from the check-in sentiment. To see how fragile that is, we biased the sentiment score by +0.3 and re-ran the models:

ModelAccuracyF1
Random Forest0.540.53
Logistic Regression0.520.49
SVM (RBF)0.510.47

Every model drops to about chance. A system that only listens to what people say is easy to throw off, which is why Aether also uses biometrics and the environment, and why the model needs regular calibration.

What the habitat does in response

  • Lighting — neutral white during the day; warm amber in the evening or when stress is elevated.
  • Sound — natural soundscapes to mask machinery noise; a soft tone when a stressed state clears.
  • Air — more airflow from the greenhouse to the sanctuary.
  • Suggestions — greenhouse care, exercise, social time or VR relaxation, matched to the person.

Privacy and limits

Data is processed on board. Links to Earth are encrypted and records are anonymized. The output is a crew-state label and a recommendation for the crew and mission planners. The system supports decisions; it does not diagnose anyone.
