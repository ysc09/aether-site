Habitat

Aether uses an ICON / Project Olympus-style regolith dome, printed on site from lunar soil, over a two-floor pressurized interior. The lower floor is a rigid metal module that arrives pre-built. The upper floor is an inflatable that expands inside the dome once it is printed.

How it gets built

  1. Rovers level the site and print a regolith pad with anchor sockets.
  2. The rigid lower module lands and is anchored to the pad.
  3. An autonomous printer builds the regolith dome over the module.
  4. The inflatable upper deck expands inside the dome.
  5. Floor plates, partitions, lighting and the external greenhouse pod are installed.

Layout

The rooms are arranged so that a crew member coming in from outside passes through the airlock, then hygiene, before reaching any living area. Crew quarters sit on the quiet upper floor, away from the airlock pumps and life-support machinery below.

Adaptive features

  • Circadian lighting — a light ring under the roof cap and room lights that shift from neutral white by day to warm amber in the evening or when stress is elevated.
  • Check-in station — a small screen, microphone and pulse pad in the wardroom for the daily check-in.
  • Crew pods — individual lighting and a bunk occupancy sensor in each pod.
  • Sensory sanctuary — a quiet alcove with a soundscape speaker, a planter and a vent fed by the greenhouse.
  • Airlock dust path — a red/green status light and the hygiene air-wash.
  • Greenhouse pod — a light sensor and grow lights; its air feeds the sanctuary.

The scale model

The model prints in 21 pieces on a Bambu A1 (about 2.6 kg of filament, by estimate). The dome shell splits into four quarters so the front can come off and show the interior; the upper floor lifts out to show the lower one. Two console trays behind the habitat hold the microcontroller and breadboards.

Things we are still working out: the snap-fit slot size has to be tuned on our printer, crew pod C sits above the airlock side with only the floor plate as a noise buffer, and we have not confirmed whether parts may extend past the 2 ft board.

Aether is a NASA HUNCH 2026–27 project by students at the Frisco ISD Career and Technical Education Center.

Dimensions, test results and power figures on this site come from our own project documents and experiments and have not been independently verified against NASA sources.
